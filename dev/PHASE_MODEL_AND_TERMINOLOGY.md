# Phase Model and Terminology

## Canonical Phase Names

### Phase 1 — Identity Discovery

**Output:** Phase 1 Identity Pitches  
**Directions:** Primary, Adjacent, Wildcard

Phase 1 identifies the campaign’s thematic center, emotional promise, style of play, broad genre and tone, and preferred creative direction. It remains intentionally system-agnostic and setting-agnostic.

### Phase 2 — Campaign Concept Development

**Output:** Phase 2 Campaign Concept Pitches

Phase 2 converts the selected identity into a concrete playable premise by adding setting circumstances, conflict, starting position, factions or forces, a recurring campaign engine, escalation, distinctive elements, and meaningful player choices.

## Working Principle

> Phase 1 discovers what the campaign wants to be.  
> Phase 2 decides what is actually happening and what the players can change.

## Terminology Rules

Use **Identity Pitch** for the current three-direction output.

Use **Campaign Concept Pitch** only for concrete Phase 2 outputs.

Use **identity direction** for Primary, Adjacent, and Wildcard.

Use **Identity Selection Record** for the authoritative client handoff into Phase 2.

Existing runtime identifiers such as `generateCampaignPitch`, `clientPitch`, and `auditPitch` remain unchanged during v0.9.x. They are legacy implementation names and should not be treated as evidence that the current output is already a finished Campaign Concept.

## Phase 1 Must Not Invent

- named settings;
- concrete crises;
- factions;
- starting situations;
- recurring campaign structures;
- fixed antagonists;
- specific game-system mechanics.

## Phase 2 May Invent

- factions and institutions;
- locations;
- a starting crisis;
- threats and pressures;
- distinctive setting elements;
- recurring campaign structures;
- bounded setting history needed to support play.

## Phase 2 Must Not Invent

- a complete plot;
- a fixed ending;
- a railroaded quest chain;
- mandatory protagonist backstories;
- excessive unrelated lore;
- genre, tone, or player-experience changes that contradict the selected identity.
